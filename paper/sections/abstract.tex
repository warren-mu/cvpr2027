\begin{abstract}
Embodied vision-language agents should gather evidence when their observations cannot yet determine the right goal, and then change or keep that goal once the evidence arrives.
Reinforcement learning makes this hard to teach: when credit is computed only in the world that happened to be realized, an agent that commits without looking is rewarded whenever its guess happens to be right.
We construct pairs of simulated worlds that are consistent with exactly the same observation history but may call for different goals, and require behavioral divergence only after discriminating evidence has entered the agent's input.
These pairs let us assign credit to evidence-seeking decisions over the worlds the agent cannot yet tell apart, which keeps the expected credit unchanged while removing the variance that comes from which world happened to be realized, and they let us supervise how the goal should be updated once the worlds can be told apart.
Every pair is checked for deterministic restoration, physical validity, a unique target in each world, an executable success witness, and pixel-level information consistency.
We compare against training on the same pairs without cross-world terms, same-world look-versus-commit branching, outcome-only reinforcement learning, and fixed and heuristic observation policies, and report success as a function of observation cost across different rates of world change.
