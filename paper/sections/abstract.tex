\begin{abstract}
An embodied agent should look before it commits when its observations cannot yet determine the goal, and once it has looked it should change or keep its goal according to what it saw.
Reinforcement learning makes the first lesson hard to teach: credit is computed in the single world an episode happens to sample, so an agent that commits without looking is rewarded whenever its guess is right.
We construct pairs of simulated worlds that differ only in the position of one object, so that they produce identical observations until the agent looks, yet may require different answers.
Running the two worlds in lockstep, we credit each pre-evidence decision against the value of committing immediately, averaged over the worlds the agent cannot yet tell apart, and after the agent has actually seen the evidence we add a consistency term that asks it to switch targets when the worlds disagree and to keep its target when they agree.
We show that, under lockstep sampling, this credit splits into two separable ingredients, stratified sampling over the hidden world and a commit-now baseline, and we test them with a controlled $2{\times}2$ design.
Every pair is validated for physical plausibility, a unique target in each world, identical inputs before the evidence step, visible evidence afterwards, and solvability within the observation budget.
\hyp{In our experiments, outcome-only PPO raises its training reward without learning to look, and its gains do not carry over to unseen rooms.
At a matched observation budget and in unseen rooms, our method commits before seeing the evidence in \tbd{} of \Look\ episodes versus \tbd{} for PPO, raises success on these episodes from \tbd{} to \tbd{}, and leaves accuracy on direct episodes and on the unchanged task intact.}
\end{abstract}
