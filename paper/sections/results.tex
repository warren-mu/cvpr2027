\subsection{Does the task require selective looking?}
\label{sec:res-trainfree}
\hyp{Never looking leaves \Look\ episodes near chance (\tbd), since the two worlds are indistinguishable at the start.
Always looking pays \tbd\ observations per episode and still commits after the evidence only if the sweep happens to reveal the anchor.
Committing as soon as the anchor is visible (privileged) reaches \tbd\ on \Look\ episodes at \tbd\ observations, which bounds what a selective policy can gain with the base model's perception.
The base model, left to decide, commits before seeing the anchor in \tbd\ of \Look\ episodes (\cref{tab:trainfree}).}

\subsection{Outcome-only RL rewards lucky guesses}
\label{sec:res-lucky}
\hyp{PPO's training reward rises steadily, but the rise comes almost entirely from \Look$\to$\Stay\ pairs, whose answer is the same in both worlds and can be given without looking, while \Look$\to$\Flip\ pairs stay near chance (\cref{fig:curves}).
Its rate of committing before seeing the anchor barely moves, and its success in held-out rooms does not improve over training.
In other words, the reward it collects is consistent with recognising training scenes and committing to their answers, which is exactly the lucky-guess pattern that outcome-only credit cannot tell apart from informed decisions.}

\subsection{Main comparison}
\label{sec:res-main}
\hyp{At a matched observation budget, our method commits prematurely in \tbd\ of \Look\ episodes, compared with \tbd\ for PPO and \tbd\ for Single-$\Qc$, and raises \Look\ success from \tbd\ (PPO) to \tbd\ (\cref{tab:main}).
It spends its extra observations on \Look\ episodes, while direct episodes are committed without looking and their accuracy is unchanged; SR on the unchanged task ($r = 0$) is not lower than PPO's.
Reading the $2{\times}2$ design, the commit-now baseline alone (Single-$\Qc$ vs.\ PPO) reduces premature commitment by \tbd, paired sampling on top of it (IS-credit vs.\ Single-$\Qc$) by a further \tbd, and the consistency term mainly changes what happens after the evidence (\cref{sec:res-post}).
On the SR--observation-cost plane, our method lies above the line traced by the training-free references and close to the privileged ``look until seen'' policy (\cref{fig:sr-cost}).}

\subsection{What happens after the evidence arrives?}
\label{sec:res-post}
\hyp{Among \Look\ episodes that commit after $\tauseen$, PPO switches correctly in \Look$\to$\Flip\ pairs \tbd\ of the time but keeps its answer in \Look$\to$\Stay\ pairs \tbd\ of the time, an asymmetry we read as a learned habit rather than a use of the evidence.
With the consistency term, both rates are \tbd\ and \tbd, closer to each other than for IS-credit alone (\cref{tab:post}).}

\subsection{Do the gains transfer, and are they robust?}
\label{sec:res-robust}
\hyp{\Cref{tab:transfer} compares \Look\ success in training rooms, in held-out training rooms, and in test rooms.
PPO is \tbd\ points better in the rooms it was trained in than in unseen rooms, consistent with memorising scene answers; for our method the gap is \tbd\ points, and held-out and test rooms agree within their confidence intervals.
The ranking of methods is unchanged on the subset of pairs that also pass the stricter physics rule and on \Look\ pairs without shadow-only divergent states.
As intended by its construction, paired sampling lowers the variance of the pre-evidence credit (IS-credit vs.\ Single-$\Qc$: \tbd\ vs.\ \tbd); we treat this as a check of the mechanism, not as evidence of better performance.}
